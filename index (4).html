Reminders

By Topic

Every reminder here is drawn only from Sunnah.com — the Qur'an and the major hadith collections, with the exact reference given.

Qur'an 1
وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍ

"And indeed, you are of a great moral character."

Sūrah Al-Qalam 68:4

Allah praises the Prophet ﷺ directly for his character in this verse. Commentators note this is one of the highest forms of commendation in the Qur'an — Allah affirming the excellence of his ﷺ character as a model for every believer to strive toward.

Hadith 2
إِنَّ خِيَارَكُمْ أَحَاسِنُكُمْ أَخْلَاقًا

"The best among you are those who have the best manners and character."

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6029 (also Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2321), via Sunnah.com — narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

This hadith was said by the Prophet ﷺ, who himself never spoke in an obscene or foul manner. It ties one's ranking as a believer directly to character rather than wealth, worship alone, or status — good akhlāq is placed at the very top.

Hadith 3
مَا شَيْءٌ أَثْقَلُ فِي مِيزَانِ الْمُؤْمِنِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ مِنْ خُلُقٍ حَسَنٍ

"Nothing is heavier on the believer's scale on the Day of Judgment than good character."

Sunan al-Tirmidhī 2002, via Sunnah.com — narrated by Abū al-Dardā' Grade: Ḥasan Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Tirmidhī)

On the Day of Judgment, deeds are weighed. This hadith teaches that good character — patience, gentleness, honesty, kindness — will outweigh many other deeds, showing how highly Islam values how we treat others day to day.

Qur'an 1
إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ تَنْهَىٰ عَنِ الْفَحْشَاءِ وَالْمُنكَرِ

"...establish prayer. Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing, and the remembrance of Allah is greater."

Sūrah Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:45

Salah isn't only a ritual — Allah tells us its purpose is to actively hold a person back from immorality and wrongdoing. A prayer that doesn't shape a person's character and behaviour outside of it is missing something essential.

Hadith 2

"Indeed the first deed by which a servant will be called to account on the Day of Resurrection is his Ṣalāh. If it is complete, he is successful and saved, but if it is defective, he has failed and lost."

Jāmiʿ at-Tirmidhī 413 (also Sunan an-Nasā'ī 465), via Sunnah.com Grade: Ḥasan (al-Tirmidhī)

Of all the deeds a person will be questioned about, Salah comes first. Scholars note this shows its weight in Islam — it is the first pillar checked, and if it is sound, other good deeds are built on that foundation.

Hadith 3
بُنِيَ الْإِسْلَامُ عَلَى خَمْسٍ

"Islam has been built on five [pillars]: testifying that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing the Ṣalāh, paying the Zakāh, making Ḥajj to the House, and fasting Ramaḍān."

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī & Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim (40 Hadith Nawawi, no. 3), via Sunnah.com — narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUmar Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

This hadith places Salah as the second pillar, right after the declaration of faith — the structural centre of a Muslim's daily life, repeated five times a day for as long as one lives.

Qur'an 1
فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُ

"So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it."

Sūrah Az-Zalzalah 99:7-8

On that Day, nothing is too small to matter — not a kind word, not a moment of harm caused. Every deed, however tiny, is shown back to its doer. Nothing is wasted, and nothing is forgotten.

Hadith 2

Allah said: "I have prepared for My righteous slaves what no eye has ever seen, nor an ear has ever heard, nor has it ever occurred to a human heart."

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 7498 (also Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2824), via Sunnah.com — narrated by Abū Hurayrah Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

Whatever we imagine of Jannah, the reality is beyond it. Its beauty isn't limited by anything we know from this world — it's described this way precisely so we don't try to box it in with worldly comparisons.

Hadith 3
حُجِبَتِ النَّارُ بِالشَّهَوَاتِ، وَحُجِبَتِ الْجَنَّةُ بِالْمَكَارِهِ

"The (Hell) Fire is surrounded by all kinds of desires and passions, while Paradise is surrounded by all kinds of disliked, undesirable things."

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6487 (also Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2822), via Sunnah.com — narrated by Abū Hurayrah Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

What feels easy and tempting often leads away from Jannah, while what feels difficult — patience, discipline, honesty when it costs you — is usually the path toward it. This hadith is a lifelong reminder to check which direction a shortcut is really taking you.

Qur'an 1
يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ

"O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you, that you may become righteous."

Sūrah Al-Baqarah 2:183

Fasting wasn't invented for this Ummah alone — it was prescribed for communities before us too. The goal named here is taqwā: God-consciousness that shapes how a person lives, not just what they eat.

Hadith 2
إِنَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ بَابًا يُقَالُ لَهُ الرَّيَّانُ

"There is a gate in Paradise called Ar-Rayyān, and those who observe fasts will enter through it on the Day of Resurrection, and none except them will enter through it."

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1896 (also Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 1152), via Sunnah.com — narrated by Sahl ibn Sa'd Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

A gate reserved only for those who fasted — no one else enters through it. It's a picture of how a private, unseen act of worship (no one can verify your fast but Allah) is rewarded with a very visible honour on that Day.

Hadith 3

"Fasting is a shield. So the person observing fasting should avoid obscene speech... By Him in Whose Hand my soul is, the smell coming out from the mouth of a fasting person is better in the sight of Allah than the smell of musk."

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1894, via Sunnah.com — narrated by Abū Hurayrah Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

Fasting isn't just abstaining from food — it's a shield against sin and bad speech too. What the world would see as a discomfort (hunger, an empty stomach) is described by the Prophet ﷺ as something beloved to Allah.

Qur'an 1
خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا

"Take, [O Muhammad], from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase..."

Sūrah At-Tawbah 9:103

Zakāh isn't just a transfer of money — the verse says it purifies the giver. Wealth held onto tightly can harden the heart; giving what's owed to others softens it and grows it, in ways money alone can't measure.

Hadith 2

"...teach them that Allah has made it obligatory for them to pay the Zakāh from their property, and it is to be taken from the wealthy among them and given to the poor among them."

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1496, via Sunnah.com — the Prophet ﷺ instructing Muʿādh ibn Jabal Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

When the Prophet ﷺ sent Muʿādh to Yemen, he gave him a clear order of priorities: first Tawḥīd, then Ṣalāh, then Zakāh. It shows Zakāh sits right alongside prayer as a defining pillar of practising Islam, not an optional extra.

Hadith 3

"Charity does not decrease wealth, no one forgives another except that Allah increases his honor, and no one humbles himself for the sake of Allah except that Allah raises his status."

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2588, via Sunnah.com — narrated by Abū Hurayrah Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

The fear that giving will leave you with less is directly addressed here — Allah's promise is the opposite. This hadith links three things together: charity, forgiveness, and humility, showing how they all raise a person rather than diminish them.

Qur'an 1
ادْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ

"And your Lord says: 'Call upon Me; I will respond to you.'"

Sūrah Ghāfir 40:60

This is a direct, personal promise from Allah — not conditional on being perfect first. The invitation is simply to call on Him, with the response guaranteed by His own words.

Hadith 2
الدُّعَاءُ هُوَ الْعِبَادَةُ

"Supplication is worship."

Jāmiʿ at-Tirmidhī 3372 (also Abū Dāwūd), via Sunnah.com — narrated by An-Nuʿmān ibn Bashīr Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Albānī)

Duʿā isn't a lesser or backup form of worship — the Prophet ﷺ identifies it as worship itself. Turning to Allah with a need, out loud or silently, is itself an act of ʿibādah, whether or not the answer looks like what was asked for.

Hadith 3

"The nearest a servant comes to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make supplication (in this state)."

Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 482, via Sunnah.com — narrated by Abū Hurayrah Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

Of all positions in Salah, sujūd is singled out as the closest a person gets to Allah. It's a practical instruction too — don't rush through prostration; it's the moment built for asking.

Qur'an 1
قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

"Say: He is Allah, [who is] One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent."

Sūrah Al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1-4

This short sūrah is described in the Sunnah as equal to a third of the Qur'an in meaning — it distills the entire concept of Tawḥīd (Allah's oneness) into four lines: no partners, no offspring, no equal.

Hadith 2

"You are going to a nation from the People of the Scripture, so let the first thing you invite them to be the Tawḥīd of Allah..."

Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 7372, via Sunnah.com — the Prophet ﷺ instructing Muʿādh ibn Jabal Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ

Before Salah, before Zakāh, before anything else — Tawḥīd comes first. This hadith shows the order of priorities in daʿwah: correct belief about Allah is the foundation everything else is built on.

Hadith 3
مَنْ كَانَ آخِرُ كَلَامِهِ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ دَخَلَ الْجَنَّةَ

"He whose last words are 'Lā ilāha illallāh' (there is no true god except Allah) will enter Jannah."

via Sunnah.com — narrated by Muʿādh ibn Jabal Grade: Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Albānī)

Tawḥīd is not just a belief recited once — it's meant to define a life so completely that it's the last thing on a person's tongue. This is part of why the shahādah is prompted gently for the dying.

Practical Guidance

Living the Sunnah

Small, authentic sunan the Prophet ﷺ practised daily — easy to carry into ordinary life.

🌅

Rising & Sleeping

Sleeping in a state of wuḍūʾ, sleeping on the right side, and remembering Allah upon waking and before sleep.

🍽️

Eating & Drinking

Saying "Bismillah" before eating, eating with the right hand, and not finding fault with food.

🚪

Entering & Leaving Home

Making duʿā when leaving the house, and greeting the household with salām on entering.

🤝

Meeting Others

Initiating salām, smiling, and being the first to extend a good greeting.

🕌

Going to the Masjid

Walking calmly, entering with the right foot, and making duʿā upon entering and leaving.

📿

Daily Dhikr

Morning and evening adhkār, istighfār throughout the day, and gratitude in small moments.

Each of these will be expanded with full authentic references soon, in shā' Allah.
